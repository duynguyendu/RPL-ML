\section{Topology Scenarios}
In this work, random topologies are used both to collect the data for
training the model and to evaluate the performance of the proposed OF.
The topologies are generated in Python and converted into \texttt{.csc}
Cooja simulation files. A topology consists of a server, clients and
overloading clients. The overloading clients are the same as clients
except they have a higher traffic rate to create unbalance section in
the network. The generator parameters and
their default values are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:topology-params}.

The server node is placed at the centre of the network and acts as the
root of the DODAG. The $N$ client nodes
are then added one at a time, each at a random position near the
existing nodes. A position is accepted only if it satisfies two constrains so that the network is connected the nodes are spread fairly evenly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Minimum distance.} The position must be at least
        $\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}R$ distance from every existing node,
        which prevents nodes from clustering.
    \item \textbf{Connectivity.} The position must lie within
        $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}R$ distance of at least $k$ existing
        nodes to ensure that nodes are not limited to a single parent and the
        links between nodes are not at the edge of radio range.
\end{itemize}

Afterward, 5\,\% of the clients are randomly selected as
\emph{overloading clients} and are
placed at least $\alpha_{\mathrm{o}}R$ distance
apart, so that their ranges do not overlap concentrate in one part of
the network.

Overall, the shape of each topology is defined by two seeds: a topology
seed $s_t$, which determines node placement, and a client seed $s_o$,
which determines which clients become overloading clients.
Figure~\ref{fig:topology-random1} shows an example random topology
with 60 nodes with transmission range $R = 50m$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \caption{Random topology generator
    parameters}\label{tab:topology-params}
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}[c]{@{}p{0.12\linewidth}p{0.12\linewidth}p{0.68\linewidth}@{}}
            \hline
            \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Default} & \textbf{Description} \\
            \hline
            $s_{\mathrm{t}}$ & -- & Topology seed, which determines the
            positions of the nodes \\
            $s_{\mathrm{o}}$ & -- & Overloading client seed, which
            determines which clients become overloading clients \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}$ & 0.4 & Minimum distance between a
            new node and every existing node, as a fraction of $R$ \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}$ & 0.8 & Maximum distance at which an
            existing node counts as a neighbour of a new node, as a
            fraction of $R$ \\
            $k$ & 2 & Minimum number of neighbours a new node must have
            within $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}R$ \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{o}}$ & 2 & Minimum distance between any two
            overloading clients, as a multiple of $R$ \\
            \hline
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
\end{table}
