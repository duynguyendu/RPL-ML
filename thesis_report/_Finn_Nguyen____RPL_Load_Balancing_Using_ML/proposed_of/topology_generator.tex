\section{Topology Scenerios}
In this work, random topologies are used both to collect the data for
training the model and to evaluate the performance of the proposed OF.
The topologies are generated in Python and converted into \texttt{.csc}
simulation files that Cooja can load. The generator parameters and
their default values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:topology-params}.

The root node is placed at the centre of the network and acts as the
server that receives packets from all clients. The $N$ client nodes
are then added one at a time, each at a random position near the
existing nodes. A candidate position is accepted only if it satisfies
two constraints. First, it must be at least $\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}R$
distance from
every existing node, which prevents nodes from clustering. Second, it
must lie within $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}R$ distance of at least $k$ existing
nodes (or of the root, for the first client). These
constraints keep the network connected, spread the nodes fairly
evenly, and ensure that no node is limited to a single parent and the
links between nodes are usable rather than at the edge of radio range.

Next, 5\,\% of the clients, rounded up, are randomly
selected as \emph{overloading clients}. They send traffic at a rate
higher than that of normal clients, representing devices that
generate heavy traffic, such as cameras. Overloading clients are
placed at least $\alpha_{\mathrm{o}}R$ distance
apart, so that their ranges do not overlap and the extra load
is not concentrated in one part of the network.

Each topology is therefore defined by two seeds: a topology
seed $s_t$, which determines node placement, and a client seed $s_o$,
which determines which clients become overloading clients.
Figure~\ref{fig:topology-random1} shows an example random topology
with 60 nodes with transmission range $R = 50m$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \caption{Random topology generator
    parameters}\label{tab:topology-params}
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}[c]{@{}p{0.12\linewidth}p{0.12\linewidth}p{0.68\linewidth}@{}}
            \hline
            \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Default} & \textbf{Description} \\
            \hline
            $s_{\mathrm{t}}$ & -- & Topology seed, which determines the
            positions of the nodes \\
            $s_{\mathrm{o}}$ & -- & Overloading client seed, which
            determines which clients become overloading clients \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}$ & 0.4 & Minimum distance between a
            new node and every existing node, as a fraction of $R$ \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}$ & 0.8 & Maximum distance at which an
            existing node counts as a neighbour of a new node, as a
            fraction of $R$ \\
            $k$ & 2 & Minimum number of neighbours a new node must have
            within $\alpha_{\mathrm{nbr}}R$ \\
            $\alpha_{\mathrm{o}}$ & 2 & Minimum distance between any two
            overloading clients, as a multiple of $R$ \\
            \hline
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
\end{table}
